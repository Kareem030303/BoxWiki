Boxing Terms

KO (Knockout)

A victory where a boxer cannot continue before the referee's count ends.

TKO (Technical Knockout)

A fight stopped by the referee, doctor, or corner because a boxer cannot safely continue.

Jab

A quick straight punch thrown with the lead hand.

Cross

A powerful straight punch thrown with the rear hand.

Hook

A curved punch thrown from the side.

Uppercut

An upward punch usually aimed at the chin or body.

Southpaw

A boxer who fights with the right hand and right foot forward.

Orthodox

A boxer who fights with the left hand and left foot forward.

Olympic Boxing - BoxWiki

🥇 Olympic Boxing

Home

About Olympic Boxing

Olympic boxing is an amateur form of boxing featured in the Summer Olympic Games. Many professional champions began their careers as Olympic boxers.

Notable Olympic Champions

  • Muhammad Ali (1960)
  • George Foreman (1968)
  • Sugar Ray Leonard (1976)
  • Oscar De La Hoya (1992)
  • Anthony Joshua (2012)

Facts

  • Olympic boxing first appeared in 1904.
  • Women’s boxing became an Olympic sport in 2012.
  • Olympic boxing follows amateur rules.
Amateur Boxing - BoxWiki

🥊 Amateur Boxing

Home

What Is Amateur Boxing?

Amateur boxing focuses on skill, technique, and experience rather than prize money. Many future world champions start as amateurs.

Major Amateur Competitions

  • Olympic Games
  • World Championships
  • Continental Championships
  • National Championships

Professional vs Amateur

  • Shorter fights
  • Different scoring
  • Focus on developing skills
  • Pathway to professional boxing
